# Audora — Progress

One entry per merged ticket, newest first. Each entry: what changed, what was verified, what still needs a real-device check.

## Optional story brain: who speaks, and how (2026-10-25, ADR-078)
- **Settings → Story brain** (6 GB phones and up) downloads Qwen3.5 4B (Apache-2.0, 2.7 GB, Wi-Fi only) from the
  app's own release in two checked parts. It reads each page's dialogue a page or two ahead of the listener, on the
  phone, and keeps its notes per book. Without it nothing changes.
- **New dependency, approved:** llama.cpp `b11887`, built from source for arm64 (`libaudora_brain.so`, every ggml
  CPU variant, the best one chosen at start). Jobs, Storage and Licences list the model.
- **Anger** is a feeling (`Feeling.ANGRY`), only from the brain. A brain feeling wins over the rules' cue words; the
  rules' tagged speakers with a known gender win over the brain's.
- Held-out right-gender voice: English 59 to 70 %, Hindi 38 to 88 %, Bengali 0 to 84 %, with 25 / 11 / 15 % wrong
  (see the ADR). Feelings heard on speech lines: 20 to 42 % right, missed 92 to 5 %.
- "What's new" (en/hi/bn) item, `WHATS_NEW_ID` 11. No `:pdf` change.
- **Verified in CI:** `StoryBrainSpecTest` (pins match, no NC licence), `StoryBrainPromptTest`, `StoryBrainAnswerTest`,
  `StoryBrainDownloadJobTest` (parts resume, join, verify, delete wins), `BrainNotesStoreTest`, `StoryBrainReaderTest`
  (spoken lines only, reads ahead, reuses notes, no model = no notes), `ReadingSourceBrainTest` (no brain = the
  rules' calls exactly), `DeliveryPlannerTest` (brain feelings, no brain = as before).

**Real-device checks (the test phone):** download the story brain on Wi-Fi; play an English, a Hindi and a Bengali
story with character voices on: the log line "Story brain page N: ... ms" shows each page well under the time it
plays, the voices follow the speakers, and angry lines sound angry. Battery over 30 minutes of listening.

## Hindi and Bengali characters found by repetition (2026-10-25, ADR-077)
- A word that opens Hindi or Bengali narration twice on a page is taken as a character ("बिरजू", "পল্টু"), so its
  quotes get its voice; a noun said once is not. Bengali sentences that open with a place, a participle or an owner
  ("ছাদে উঠে", "মাছের দাম শুনে") now find the real subject.
- Held-out Hindi right speaker 7 % to 38 %; a second held-out set: Hindi 0 to 80 %, Bengali 0 to 83 %. One wrong
  voice in that set (see the ADR). Bengali names still get no voice of their own (no gender from the verb).
- "What's new" (en/hi/bn) item, `WHATS_NEW_ID` 9. No `:pdf` change. No new dependency.
- **Verified in CI:** `QuoteCarryTest` (a repeated Hindi name is a character, a noun is not), `SpeakerTagsTest`
  (Bengali openers), `ExpressiveEvalTest` (held-out floors, new second held-out set).

**Real-device checks (the test phone):** a Hindi story with Supertonic and character voices on: the two main
characters' lines get their own voices after their names have opened two sentences.

## Feelings you can hear (2026-10-25, ADR-076)
- **Pitch per feeling on every voice:** sad lines are 1.5 semitones lower, lively and surprised 2 higher, scared 1.5,
  loud 1, capped at 2.5. Only the quoted words move; narration around them stays plain. Voices that act feelings
  themselves (Rasa, studio) are not shifted. Made by resampling (`PcmAudio.pitched`), with the voice asked for the
  speed that keeps the words' length.
- **Stronger speed, loudness and pauses** per feeling (`DeliveryConfig` effects).
- "What's new" (en/hi/bn) leads with it, `WHATS_NEW_ID` 8. No `:pdf` change. No new dependency.

  (sad lower, lively higher, plain and news unchanged, a storybook within the cap).

**Real-device checks (the test phone):** with "Read to suit the book" on, play a story with a sad and a happy line in

## Character voices that find the characters (2026-10-25, ADR-075)
The maintainer heard character voices fail most of the time. Measured first, then fixed.
- **A marked story set** (`research/expressive-eval/`): 20 stories in English, Hindi and Bengali, each sentence
  marked with its speaker and feeling, plus a held-out set never tuned on. `ExpressiveEvalTest` scores the app's
  reading of them and fails if a score drops, a character gets the other gender's voice, or a feeling is read as
  its opposite. Reports land in `playback/build/expressive/`.
- **Characters found:** single-quoted dialogue, one-word lines, Hindi and Bengali tags read subject first (sadhu
  verbs too), names and role words that tell gender, the narration's subject for an untagged line, never the person
  a line calls by name (the one it calls answers next), English names as printed. Right speaker 17/11/26 % →
  87/84/100 % (en/hi/bn); right-gender voice 29/11/0 % → 85/88/100 % on the tuned set, 22/0/0 % → 59/7/0 % held out. English knows ~3,000 more given names from public-domain
  birth records.
- **"!" alone reads emphatic, not happy.** Opposite feelings 5 → 0 on the tuned set.
- "A voice for each character" is labelled experimental; What's new lists the changes; studio audio renders again.

**Real-device checks (test phone):** a story with single-quoted dialogue in English with Kitten, a Hindi story with
Piper Pratham and a Bengali story with Rasa: characters switch voices, men and women are not swapped, "Get out!"
does not sound cheerful in Bengali.
## Speed 1: the phones run an optimised build (2026-10-25, ADR-072)
- **The dev APK is no longer a debug build.** `audora-dev.apk` is now the new `dev` build type: release settings
  (not debuggable, R8, native code at -O2) with the dev key and the debug app id, so it updates in place. The
  debug build stays on the release page as `listenlock-debug.apk`, a fallback. Release has R8 on too
  (`app/proguard-rules.pro`, no renaming).
- **Library covers fill in faster:** a cover already saved is decoded without queueing behind a page being drawn.
- **Word highlight** checks the voice's position 30 times a second instead of on every frame (up to 144 times a second on fast screens).
- **Speed is measured:** CI times debug against dev on the emulator on every push (run summary). Share logs now has
  one `Audora.RenderSpeed` line per sentence (render time, audio length, rtf, and `first` when a voice loaded).
  Phone steps: `docs/PERFORMANCE.md`.
**Real-device checks:** install the new `audora-dev.apk` over the old one (books and settings stay). Open a
book, play, change voice, scroll the library, then Share logs. If anything crashes that did not before, install
`listenlock-debug.apk` and send the logs.

## Release hardening 4: app lock and Continue (2026-10-25, ADR-061)
From the pre-release security review.
- **The app lock covers open sheets, dialogs and menus:** the lock screen is now drawn in its own full-screen window,
  opened after the content's, so a notes sheet or a book's details left open when the app locked is hidden behind
  it instead of staying readable and tappable on top. Back on the lock screen leaves the app. "Hide app content"
  still applies (the window inherits the activity's security flag).
- **Only Audora's own notification can continue a stopped export or copy:** MainActivity is exported, so any app
  could open it with a book id and start writing that book to shared Audiobooks or Documents. The Continue action
  now carries a one-time random token kept in app storage (`ContinueTokens`); without it nothing starts and the
  attempt is logged.
- **Verified in CI:** `ContinueTokensTest` (once only, wrong or missing token refused, one token per job).

**Real-device checks:** turn on App lock, open a book's Notes sheet, leave the app for over 30 s, come back:
only the lock screen shows; unlock and the sheet is still there.

## Release hardening 3: voice installs (2026-10-25)
From the pre-release review.
- **A voice deleted while it downloads stays deleted:** catalog voices now install through the same gate as the
  OCR and punctuation models (`VoiceFiles.installGate`): a delete marks every download started before it as stale,
  so its install is skipped and its zip removed. Before, the voice came back, could become the reading voice again,
  and kept its space.
- **The bundled voice's espeak-ng data is copied by one engine at a time:** playback, studio and quiz each load
  the bundled voice; two copies into one staging folder could leave a folder missing files that its marker called
  complete (`AssetDirectoryInstaller`, one process-wide lock).
- **A full phone keeps the downloaded voice:** when unpacking fails for lack of space, the verified zip stays and
  the message says to free space; the next try installs it without downloading again. Any other failure still
  deletes it.
- **Verified in CI:** `VoiceDownloadJobTest` (delete during download), `AssetDirectoryInstallerTest` (two installs at
  once), `VoiceArchiveInstallerTest` (full phone told apart from a broken zip).

**Real-device checks:** start a Hindi voice download, delete it from Voices before it finishes: it does not
come back.

## Release hardening 2: words read wrong (2026-10-25)
From the pre-release review. Speech text changes, so STUDIO_AUDIO_VERSION is 3; :pdf is untouched.
- **"the book I read" is no longer "the book one read":** after book, part, class, lesson and the other numbering
  words, a lone "I" followed by a verb (an auxiliary, a common verb, any "-ed" word) stays the pronoun. Before
  anything else it is still the numeral ("Part I.", "Type I diabetes", "Book I of the Iliad"), and after "World
  War" it always is (`RomanNumeralRule`).
- **Hindi and Bengali say a minus sign:** "-5 डिग्री" is "माइनस पाँच डिग्री" ("মাইনাস" in Bengali); a dash
  between numbers stays a range. Equations keep the formal "ऋण".
- **Map scales:** "1:50,000" is no longer read as a clock time.
- **A book's pronunciation fixes apply from its first sentence:** `BookContext.open` returns once the fixes are
  loaded (one collection, so an edit made while the book opens is not missed). Before, up to 40 sentences were
  rendered without them.
- **Pitch:** a book's pitch saved under the old range is brought into ±15% when the book opens (ADR-071 said so;
  the code did not). The slider no longer jumps back while dragging when the service's echo of an earlier step
  arrives late. The - and + buttons are "One step deeper/higher", so TalkBack no longer gives them the same names
  as the Deeper and Higher presets.
- What's new (id 3) lists these.
- **Verified in CI:** `EnglishNormalizerTest` (pronoun and numeral "I", map scale), `IndicNormalizerTest` (minus),
  `BookContextTest` (open waits for the fixes; old pitch clamped), `JobsAndPitchScreensTest`.

**Real-device checks:** read a novel page with "the book I read": hear "I". Add a fix to a book, close it,
reopen it: the first sentence uses the fix. In a Hindi text with "-5", hear "माइनस पाँच".

## Release hardening 1: crash guards (2026-10-25)
From the pre-release review (all modules). No reading output changes, so PIPELINE_VERSION stays.
- **One odd page no longer ends playback:** any defect while building a page's sentences (a broken invariant, a
  layout walk that recurses too deep) becomes "page could not be read", which listening already skips and logs
  (`PdfBook.contained`). Before, the exception went past the typed result and stopped the service.
- **Damaged PDFs open as "damaged", never crash:** a file with zero pages is refused at open; PDFium errors other
  than password and I/O close the file and report it damaged; a table of contents that loops back on itself is read
  as no chapters (`StackOverflowError` caught, depth capped at 32). The resume locator returns nothing for a book
  with no pages instead of throwing.
- **Headset Play after the app was closed** always answers Media3 (resumption runs through `launchedFuture`), and
  the playback service's scope logs a failed task instead of ending the process. Up next treats a start that
  throws like a book that cannot be opened and tries the next one.
- **Made-audio player starts only from a tap:** when Android restores the screen after closing the app, the audio
  is loaded paused (C7). Playing a voice note catches every MediaPlayer failure and releases the player.
- **Verified in CI:** `PdfBookTest` (zero pages; a throwing splitter fails only its page), `ResumeLocatorTest`
  (zero pages), `QueueAdvanceTest` (a throwing start is skipped).

**Real-device checks:** open a Made-audio file, swipe the app away from Recents, reopen it from Recents:
the player shows paused. Play a book, press Play on a Bluetooth headset after swiping the app away: it resumes.

## Rows that never squeeze their titles; pitch that works like speed (2026-10-25, ADR-071)
- **Option rows:** a value shares the title's line only when both fit whole; otherwise it goes on its own line under
  the title and wraps once ("English voice" was squeezed to one letter per line by a long voice name). Every
  settings and sheet row built on `LinkOption`, `ChoiceOption` or `OptionBlock` gets it, and the collections
  sheet's Rename and Delete do too (`TitleAndTrailing`).
- **Pitch:** presets like speed's (Deeper, A bit deeper, Natural, A bit higher, Higher) under the slider. The
  slider is heard while it moves. Natural sits in the middle of the track, the range is ±15% (was 0.8 to 1.25,
  where voices sounded like cartoons), and a line under the title says speed never changes it.
  The slider ticks at each step like the speed slider. What's new (id 2) lists both changes.
- **Verified in CI:** `OptionRowsLayoutTest` renders the cast rows with a long voice name, link rows and the pitch
  block at font 1×, 1.3× and 2× and fails if any line breaks inside a word (screenshots `option-rows-font-*`). The
  pitch device test taps presets, + and Natural. Unit tests cover the new range and preset rules.

**Real-device checks:** Speech → Cast voices with a long voice picked, at normal and largest font: the
title stays whole and the voice name sits under it. Play a book, open Speech, drag Pitch: the voice changes while
dragging. Tap A bit higher, then Natural.

## Sentence menu fixes from phone tests: passage highlights, sharing them, a working "This sounded wrong" (2026-10-24)
- **Highlight a passage:** the sentence menu has "Highlight from here…": a bar says "Tap the sentence where it ends",
  and the next sentence tapped (on this page or up to 10 pages on, or before the start) ends one highlight over all of
  them. Pages between are read without OCR, so a scanned page not read yet refuses the passage and says why.
  `HighlightPlacement` places such a highlight on every page it covers: at the pipeline it was saved with by its
  offsets (sentences without words such as `* * *` included), across pages by its words (the start page's tail begins
  the quote, the end page's head ends it, pages between sit inside it). Stored as one highlight (no schema change);
  Remove on any of its sentences removes all of it.
- **Share as audio** on a highlighted sentence shares the whole passage (up to 2 minutes, `ClipConfig.passageMaxMs`;
  10 pages), not the tapped sentence to its paragraph's end (`ClipPassage`).
- **This sounded wrong** used to only write a log line. After the reason it now offers what fixes it with the
  settings in use (`SoundedWrongFixes`): a wrong word opens "Fix how a word sounds"; a table offers the other table
  modes; a note offers footnotes on or off and skipping [bracketed notes]; a pause offers clause pauses and the
  sentence pause; junk offers "Skip unreadable text" when it is off. A setting applies at once; "Read this sentence
  again" checks it. The report still goes to the log.
- **Hints:** every sentence-menu item has one line under it saying what it is for (voice note, Look up as the offline
  dictionary, and so on), in English, Hindi and Bengali.
- `SentenceMenuTest` (device) checks the hints, the passage actions, the range bar and the fixes dialog, and saves
  screenshots to the `player-screens-<run>` artifact. No `:pdf` change (PIPELINE_VERSION 12).

**Real-device checks:** long-press a sentence, "Highlight from here…", turn a page, tap a sentence: the band
covers both pages. Long-press inside it: "Share as audio" sends the passage. Long-press a table row, "This sounded
wrong", "A table", "Tables: read every row", then "Read this sentence again".

## Large scanned books never hang the app (2026-10-23, ADR-054)
- **Root cause:** a scanned page's OCR (up to a minute) ran under the book's lock, and leaving the reader or stopping playback closed the book on the main thread, which waited for that lock: an "isn't responding" dialog. - **Fixes:** `PdfBook.close()` never waits (the document closes as the page being read lets go); page reads in the reader and the player are interruptible and OCR stops between its steps; one OCR page in memory at a time across the app; running out of memory on a page fails that page instead of crashing; search never runs OCR (scanned pages join it once shown or read aloud).
- **Feedback:** a page still loading after 3 s says it is being read with OCR and that pages can still be turned (English, Hindi, Bengali).
- **Guards:** debug builds log a main thread stuck for 2 s with its stack to the shared logs (`StallWatchdog`), plus StrictMode in logcat. Tests: `PdfBookCloseTest`, `RenderedPageOcrStopTest`, `StallWatchdogTest`, and the emulator's `LargeScannedBookTest` on a generated 700-page scanned PDF. No `:pdf` output change (PIPELINE_VERSION 12, golden unchanged).

(or "जारी"/"क्रमशः"/"ক্রমশ" bracketed or ending the caption) stands before it: when the header row is repeated (more than half of its cells and at least two), every unit of it is marked `TableRowInfo.continued` with `rowsBefore`; when the next page opens with data (a serial number continuing the last row, or cells written like the column's data with no header years: a span such as "2018-19" or a year with more digits than its column's figures names years; a plain year as long as its column's figures, as in "State | 2020" over "Goa | 5352", decides nothing, so only a running serial number carries such a row on), a continued summary unit of no length is set at the first row's start and that row becomes data row 0 under the carried header. Every continued table therefore opens with a continued `TABLE_SUMMARY`. A table whose own first row is data (on its own page, carried or not: its serial number runs on into the next row, or it is written like the rows in every number column and holds no year, so word headers such as "Name | Age | City" and year headers such as "District | 2011 | 2021" stay headers) has that row read as data row 0 under a summary of no length, not continued and with no headers, so its rows are numbered as heard and it hands on no header; consumers tell a summary of no length by `charEnd == charStart`, never by `continued`. `PageReading.continuesPreviousPage` (also `BookText.reading`) says the page opens inside the sentence the page before broke off (at least three words, ending on a letter, comma or dash, the next page in the same kind and lower case; when either side is in a script without capitals, the layout decides whatever the next page opens with ("RBI", "2019"): the last line, read upright, must reach the usual right edge of its column (the median of at least two other lines') and the next page's first line must not be indented; a page opening with an item number ("2.", "3)", "(4)") never continues, while a bare number before a word ("15 अगस्त को…") may). On the salary list (13 pages) all seven continuation pages carry their table; on the 57 kind PDFs 40 page breaks carry (7 tables, 33 sentences), from 56 before these rules; the dropped ones are new paragraphs, form fields, a chapter heading, running heads, print slugs and garbled lines of broken fonts.
- **"Note:" asides on scanned pages:** a body paragraph opening with a note label ("Note:", "नोट -", "टिप्पणी:", "দ্রষ্টব্য:", "N.B.", "বিঃদ্রঃ") is an aside on a scanned page too, by the text layer's rule (`NoteFinder.opensWithNoteLabel`); two Hindi notes on the test scans. ## Smart speed (plan item 10; 2026-10-18)
- **Smart speed** (speech sheet, sound section; off by default, ADR-049): past the voice model's own top speed (1.5×), the rest of the speed comes from an uneven stretch of the voice instead of the player's even one. Silences and held vowels are shortened most, onsets (consonants) least, and the sentence still comes out exactly as long as the speed asks; the pitch is kept (WSOLA). Audora's own pauses keep their length; the audiobook export is unaffected.
  - `SmartStretch` (`:speech`), tunables in `SmartSpeedConfig`; `SpeechStyleConfig.playerSpeed(style)` and `voiceStretch(style)` split the speed; the render-ahead window and the render speed tracker count the stretch.
  - Review fixes: silence is judged against the sentence's own loud level (a quiet voice keeps its weak consonants); a frame next to an onset is spared like it; windows line up by normalised correlation searched outward from their place (8 ms, so 80 Hz voices don't warble), and a near-silent window keeps its place; the speed spread is weighted by frame length; the last 4 ms fade out so no click lands in the pause. Changing speed below the model's top speed no longer restarts the sentence (`SpeechStyleConfig.asHeard`); a clause pause the stretch would cut is kept. Settings key `smartSpeed`.
  - "What did it say?" with smart speed on renders the sentence again at the slower speed rather than slowing the stretched voice (no double time-stretch; it starts after that sentence's render time).

## Maths read aloud (plan item 66; 2026-10-17)
- With equations set to be read as written, an equation is now read the way a teacher reads it: "x² + y² = z²" is "x squared plus y squared equals z squared", "x^{n+1}" "x to the power n plus one", "a_{n-1}" "a sub n minus one", "√(x+1)" "the square root of x plus one", "(a+b)/(c+d)" "the fraction a plus b over c plus d", "d²y/dx²" "d squared y by d x squared", "∂f/∂x" "partial f by partial x", "|x − 3|" "the absolute value of x minus three", "n!" "n factorial", "sin²x" "sine squared x", "ln(xy)" "natural log of x y", "lim x→0" "the limit as x approaches zero", "f′(x)" "f prime of x", "½" "one half", "…" "and so on". Letters run together ("xy", "dx") are read as letters, and a one-letter group "(x)" is the letter, never the roman numeral ten. - How: `MathStructure` in `:speech`, a fixed order of rules run before `MathSymbols` on equation text only (the `EQUATION` units RI-7a tags). No `:pdf` change, no pipeline version bump, no new data (the rules are code). The default equation mode is unchanged ("Equation 3"), so the listening score doesn't move.
- Tests: `MathStructureTest` (table-driven, English, Hindi and Bengali), `MathSymbolsTest` unchanged and green.
- **Gaps:** the online model for notation the rules can't parse (nested groups, matrices, sums with limits, anything a PDF flattens such as a superscript printed as a plain "x2") waits on ADR-037 (call 1).

**Real-device checks (final phone session):** with "Read equations as written", listen to a page of the maths paper in the listening corpus and a Hindi or Bengali textbook page with equations.

## Pauses in unpunctuated scans — the opt-in punctuation model (RI-11; 2026-10-17, ADR-035)
- **What it does:** Settings → "Pauses in unpunctuated scans (English)" downloads a 7.5 MB model (Edge-Punct-Casing's English CNN-BiLSTM, int8, Apache-2.0, run by sherpa-onnx's online punctuation API); downloading is the opt-in and delete removes it. While it is installed, a sentence from an OCR page that is English, 40 words or longer and has no `. ? ! । ॥` inside gets the model's commas and question marks, so the voice pauses there (RI-6's clause pauses). Everything else, including every text-layer page and any sentence that already has a full stop, is never sent to it (`RunOnPunctuation`, `PunctuationConfig`).
- **Why so narrow:** measured on our English texts with punctuation stripped (the eval notes are kept with the project research, outside the repo), sentence ends scored F1 0.33 for this model and 0.27 for the 75 MB CT-Transformer, against the ticket's 0.85; about two of every three full stops land mid-sentence. So its full stops (and `! ; :`) are read as commas: a wrong one costs a short pause, not a broken sentence. The user's card offered "long runs only" (built), "all scanned text" and "rules only".
- **Safety:** only marks are taken from the model. Its words are matched one for one against the original; any changed, split or dropped word keeps the sentence as written, and the original spelling and case are kept (an acronym it lower-cases still reads as before). A model that fails to load or answer is logged once and the text reads as written. Logs carry the page and sentence, never the text.
- **Download:** pinned like the voices (SHA-256, resumable, WorkManager, `PunctuationModels`, `PunctuationDownloadJob`, `PunctuationFiles`, `noBackupFilesDir/punctuation/<sha12>`); only the zip's two model files are unpacked, capped. The zip is built by `scripts/build_voice_mirror.py` from `speech/models.json`'s new `models` list (tar.bz2 sources supported) and published to the `voices` release by the mirror workflow on dev, so the download works once this is merged. Credit added to the licences screen. The playback service and "Save as audiobook" both use it.
- Tests: `RunOnPunctuationTest`, `PunctuationDownloadTest` (pins match models.json, safe unpack, resume, give-up), `ReadingSetupTest`, the mirror script's tarball and `models` tests. Golden and listening scores unchanged (no `:pdf` change; the listening corpus is born-digital, so the model never runs there). No new dependency (sherpa-onnx already ships the API).
- **Gaps:** English only (no Hindi or Bengali punctuation model exists offline in sherpa-onnx); a danda or full stop from a scanned line end is the PDF pipeline's to restore, not this model's.

**Real-device checks (final phone session):** download the model from Settings and delete it; a scanned English page with no full stops (e.g. a photocopied notice) sounds less breathless with the model and unchanged without; a punctuated scan and a normal PDF sound the same either way; render speed on the Moto stays ahead of playback on a dense scanned page.

## Reading intelligence, step 7 — tables on scanned pages (RI-4a part 2; 2026-10-17, ADR-035)
- **Ruled tables rebuilt from their rules:** the lines ruled on a scanned page are found in the page image (`RuleFinder`), and the table's rows and columns come from them (`RuledTables`), so a government list or form that Tesseract read cell by cell, a column at a time, is read row by row like a text-layer table. A "|" read from a rule is left out; a footer or title across the grid stays text; a heading inside a page frame is not taken for a cell.
- **Borderless tables from OCR's rows:** lines read side by side are joined back into rows (Tesseract often splits a table into blocks of columns) and built by the same code as text-layer tables; prose paragraphs, two-column prose and cells wrapped over several lines are kept out, so they are read whole.
- **Measured** on the four labelled tables of the corpora OCR'd from 300 dpi renders: 145 of 145 cells in the right row and column, 106 exactly as printed (the rest are OCR misreads of ×, −, superscripts). The six UDHR scans are unchanged.
- **Book kinds (RI-10)** re-measured with scanned pages read this way: 49 of 57, from 50. A Bengali staff-transfer notice labelled a gazette is mostly tables (56% of its units are now table rows) and reads as a form.
- `PIPELINE_VERSION` stays 10 (not on `dev` yet; only OCR'd pages change). Golden 0.9268 and listening 0.9783 unchanged (no ruled scan in either). OCR cache format v3 (rules), older entries still decode. No new dependency.
- Tests: `RuleFinderTest` (a grid, text, a filled bar and short strokes, a faint rule, a double rule, a broken rule, the switch), `RuleConfigTest`, `TableConfigTest`, `OcrTablesTest` (a ruled table read cell by cell with a rule glyph, a footer row across the grid, one tall row of ruled columns, a borderless table read as two blocks, two-column prose), `HocrTest`, `OcrTextCodecTest`.

**Known limits:** a cell Tesseract never read stays empty (serial numbers in thick-ruled forms); a table continued from the previous page takes its first row as its header; a letterhead set as two aligned blocks reads as a small table; two prose columns run together by Tesseract stay run together.

**Real-device checks:** open a scanned government list or form with ruled cells and check that rows are read as rows; note the first-read time of a scanned page (rules add well under a second).

## Reading intelligence, step 5 — what kind of book it is (RI-10; 2026-10-17, ADR-035)
- **`PdfBook.documentKind`** reads a dozen pages spread over a book (OCR'd where the app would OCR them) and names it a novel, paper, textbook, gazette, form or general, so playback can pick its table, footnote, citation and skim defaults per kind (the player side, app basics). The listener's choice always wins. Call it once per book off the main thread: it costs up to 12 page reads.
- **How it decides** (`DocumentKindClassifier`, thresholds in `DocumentKindConfig`): table rows or digits → form; citations, figures and equations in a short document → paper; official words in English, Hindi and Bengali → gazette; headings and lists in a longer book → textbook; long sentences with no structure → novel; else general. Junk text (below the gibberish gate's threshold) is not counted.

Fixing that means reading headings and tables from OCR word boxes, which changes OCR'd units (PIPELINE_VERSION bump).
- `PIPELINE_VERSION` unchanged (units are unchanged). Golden 0.9268 and listening 0.9783 unchanged. No new dependency.
- Tests: `DocumentKindClassifierTest` (every kind, rates over all pages, signals counted from units and raw text, junk left out, sample spread), `DocumentKindConfigTest`, `KindLabelsTest` (the golden excerpts classified as labelled, with each book's real page count).

**Real-device checks:** none yet (nothing in the app calls it until the player side stores and uses it).

## Reading intelligence, step 4 — footnotes and note markers (RI-5a; 2026-10-17, ADR-035)
- **Note markers fall silent:** small raised numbers and symbols after a word or punctuation ("both.¹", "Abbott,³", "Gal∗") are left out of what the voice is given, the way "[4]" citations already were. Exponents are read: "x²", "10⁻²¹", "χ²", "600 deg²", "(x+1)²", and subscripts ("B₁"). A switch in `NoteConfig` keeps markers if a reading mode ever wants them.
- **Footnotes tagged** (`FOOTNOTE`, with the note's number in `noteRef`): the small lines at the foot of a column, split into one note per number; a note carried over from the previous page is tagged without a number. They stay where they are printed, so playback reads them at the end of the page as now; RI-5b (app basics) adds "Footnotes: read / skip".
- **"Note:" paragraphs tagged** (`ASIDE`): "Note:", "NOTE –", "N.B.", "टिप्पणी:", "नोट:", "দ্রষ্টব্য:", "টীকা:".
- `PIPELINE_VERSION` stays 10 (not on `dev` yet). Golden 0.9268 unchanged; listening 0.9782 → 0.9783 (the forall x footnote page no longer hears its "1"). `docs/LISTENING.md` regenerated. No new dependency.
- Tests: `NoteFinderTest` (markers vs exponents in a table, subscripts, ordinals, numbered footnotes, a carried-over note, a stray table cell and a smaller quotation that are not footnotes, asides in three languages, the switch), `NoteConfigTest`, and `pdf/golden/notes/labels.tsv` (both corpora: every marker and footnote, nothing else). Counted outside the repository on the ticket's four books (arXiv TraceMonkey, the LIGO paper with about 1,000 authors, the CERC Hindi–English gazette, 60 pages of forall x): 1,172 of 1,172 markers silenced, 0 of 18 exponents and fractions taken for one, a footnote found for every footnote marker but one (the LIGO paper's, see below).

**Known limits:** scanned pages: OCR gives no sizes, so markers there are still read ("both.1") and footnotes are body text. A marker printed at full size (only raised) is read. Small print at a column's foot without a number (a journal's licence lines, a print slug) is tagged as a footnote too, so "Skip footnotes" will skip it, which is what a listener wants. A footnote that cleanup takes for page furniture is dropped as before (the LIGO paper's "Full author list given at the end").

**Real-device checks:** open forall x page 13 (or any book with footnotes) and check that "never both." is read without "one", and the footnote is read after the page's text.

## Reading intelligence, step 3b — tables kept as rows and columns (RI-4a; 2026-10-17, ADR-035)
- **Tables are kept, not dropped:** each table cleanup finds becomes a header unit (`TABLE_SUMMARY`, the header row) and one `TABLE_ROW` unit per data row, with each cell as a span of the row's real text and the header texts alongside (`TableRowInfo`). Works on English, Hindi and Bengali text layers, legacy fonts included.
- **Grid from geometry** (`TableGrid`): cells wrapped over two lines stay in their row, a rank printed once beside two records goes with the first, a header over two or three lines is one header, a header or total row that layout cut off from the table joins it, and a chart's letter-by-letter axis titles are not taken for a table (dropped as before). A title across the table's edge stays text.
- **Playback placeholder until RI-4b** (`TablePlaceholder`, marked in code): rows are skipped and a table is announced by its size ("4 rows, 5 columns.", in Hindi and Bengali too), so nothing is read that was not read before, plus the announcement.
- `PIPELINE_VERSION` stays 10 (not on `dev` yet). Golden 0.9268 unchanged (table units are not scored); listening 0.9775 → 0.9782 (the tables page now hears its two tables announced). `docs/QUALITY.md` and `docs/LISTENING.md` regenerated. No new dependency.
- Tests: `TableGridTest` (wrapped cell, rank spanning rows, two-line header, chart), `TableConfigTest`, updated cleanup tests, and `pdf/golden/tables/labels.tsv` typed from the rendered pages: 4 tables (forall x connectives, the physimaging paper's Tables 1–3), 145 of 145 cells right. Tables we cannot redistribute, scored by eye outside the repository: the Bengali college result list, 67 of 72 cells (93%; the misses are a rank and grade spanning three records put on the middle record, and one name the legacy-font map spells wrong); the Hindi Jal Shakti report reads its state-wise tables row for row with their totals, and its contents pages as number, title and page.

**Known limits:** tables on scanned pages are not read yet (Tesseract gives every cell its own block; a block-grid path is the next step of RI-4a). Two columns printed less than 1 em apart are read as one (the Jal Shakti report's second funds table: serial number and state name in one cell). Ruled tables whose rows a cell spans vertically centred can attach that cell to the middle row. A heading inside a table (between two sub-tables) stays in a row. Ruled lines are not used (ticket scope).

**Real-device checks:** open a PDF with a table (the physimaging paper, page 2) and check that the voice says "10 rows, 7 columns." where the table is and then reads on after it.

## Reading intelligence, step 3a — captions and equations (RI-7a; 2026-10-17, ADR-035)
- **Captions tagged** (`CAPTION`): every sentence of a figure or table caption, in English, Hindi and Bengali ("Fig. 2.", "Table 3:", "चित्र 4:", "সারণি ২ ।"), when the label is set apart (a delimiter, nothing or a capital after it, or a heading, bold or smaller line). "Fig. 2 shows…" in the text stays body. A caption that layout ran into the paragraph above it ("…format takes. / Table 1. Useful specifiers") is split off.
- **Display equations tagged** (`EQUATION`): lines of maths set off from the text (an equation number, an indent, or a block of their own). Maths inside a sentence stays in the sentence; code, garbled chart labels and broken-font Bengali are not taken for maths.
- Unit boundaries kept: a caption's label stays its own sentence and each equation line its own unit, so positions saved on older builds still resume. Playback gives the kinds their readings in RI-7b (announce or skip; a run of equation lines read as one equation).
- `PIPELINE_VERSION` 10 (a display line now ends the sentence before it). Golden 0.9268 and listening 0.9775 unchanged; `docs/QUALITY.md` regenerated. No new dependency.
- Tests: table-driven caption and equation cases (`FurnitureFinderTest`), `FurnitureConfig`, and `pdf/golden/furniture/labels.tsv`: all 36 labelled captions and equations of both corpora tagged, nothing else. Checked by eye on 12 more test PDFs (arXiv papers, forall x, Pro Git, Hindi and Bengali government documents): no wrong caption.

**Known limits:** numbers in tables that cleanup does not find yet are tagged as equations (RI-4a reads tables). A caption that layout ran together with the body text after it takes that text with it. On scanned pages only captions with a delimiter are found (OCR gives no sizes or bold).

**Real-device checks:** none yet (the kinds are read as body text until RI-7b).

## Reading intelligence, step 2 — gibberish scoring (RI-2a; 2026-10-17, ADR-035)
- **Every sentence gets a `speakScore`** (0–1) from `SpeakabilityScorer`: OCR word confidence, the share of words in the language's word list, how word-like the letters are (a letter-trigram model trained from the list), clean tokens (no symbol soup, no "PTfuqT", no Devanagari glued to Latin digits) and words in one script. Nothing is skipped yet: RI-2b (app basics) adds "Skip unreadable text" in playback.
- **Word lists bundled, not downloaded:** `scripts/speakability/build_models.py` builds them from wordfreq 3.1.1 (data CC BY-SA 4.0): English 78k, Hindi 24k, Bengali 93k words, 0.9 MB in `:pdf`'s resources. Loaded per language on first use; a missing list is logged once and that language scores without them.
- **OCR confidence kept per word** (`OcrWord.confidence` from Tesseract's hOCR, OCR cache format 2; format 1 pages still decode, without confidences).
- **Tuned on 1,234 labelled lines** (`pdf/golden/speakability/labels.tsv`: bad OCR layers, wrong-language and upside-down OCR, a 60 dpi scan, a broken Bengali font map, against clean books, scans and table cells): 91.0% of junk lines caught, 1.4% of real lines flagged (done-when: 90% and 2%); 84% / 1.8% with each document held out. Under 5 ms a sentence (`SpeakabilityLabelsTest`).
- `PIPELINE_VERSION` unchanged (9): scores only. Golden (0.9268) and listening (0.9775) unchanged. No new dependency (wordfreq is a build-time source of data, like Tesseract for the recordings).

**Known limits:** visual-order Bengali whose common words come out right still scores above the gate on about a quarter of its lines (OCR repair, RI-3, and the confidence badge, RI-9). Labels are this thread's reading, not the maintainer's. The wordfreq CC BY-SA credit is on the "Voices & licences" screen.

## P1-04 — Playback service (2026-10-15)
- **Reading aloud works end to end:** the reader's Play / tap-a-sentence sends `PLAY_FROM` to `AudoraPlaybackService` (Media3 `MediaSessionService`, foreground type `mediaPlayback`), which opens the book through the library's file grant, extracts pages on one background thread (PDFium, P1-02) about a page ahead of the playhead, and reads sentence by sentence. Notification, lock screen, headset and Bluetooth buttons come from Media3's session (play/pause, previous/next sentence). Speed is the player's own (Sonic, C6).
- **Never ENDED while text remains (C5):** each playlist item is one sentence (a WAV served by `TtsDataSource`, which blocks until the render queue has it), and the playlist always ends with a "more is coming" placeholder that blocks until the next page is appended; only after the last page is it removed, so ENDED means the book is finished.
- **Render queue** (`RenderAheadQueue`): one worker thread renders the playing sentence plus 3 ahead (`QueueConfig.prefetchUnits`) with P1-03's `SpeechEngine`; a sentence that leaves the window mid-render is cancelled. Played sentences stay in an 8 MiB in-memory cache so skip-back is instant. Deviation from Build Spec §6 (20 MB disk LRU), recorded as ADR-016: memory only, because a disk cache of a user's book audio is one more thing to clean up and gains little for a sentence-level skip-back; revisit if skip-back of more than ~3 min is wanted.
- **Seek and voice change rebuild the queue:** every start, seek or `SET_VOICE` opens a new render epoch (new queue, new media ids `audora://book/<id>/e/<epoch>/…`); loads from an older epoch fail fast and are never played. A voice change restarts the current sentence in the new voice. English uses the bundled Piper voice by default, Hindi/Bengali the phone's best offline system voice (P1-03).
- **Recovery** (`PlaybackRecovery`): a sentence the engine cannot render is skipped (reported in the session extras), but 3 in a row stop reading with an error instead of skipping a whole book; a dead engine stops at once. Media3's stuck-buffering error (10 min, S3 finding 3) restarts the render queue at the current sentence, a second time on the same sentence skips it; "stuck" while Android suppresses audio (a phone call) just retries. Other player errors retry once.
- **Sleep timer runs in the service:** minutes (counted on `elapsedRealtime`, so deep sleep counts; when it runs out the sentence playing is finished first) or end of page (the player's pause-at-end-of-item).
- **Contract for the UI** (`playback.contract.PlaybackContract`): custom commands `PLAY_FROM`, `SET_VOICE`, `SET_SLEEP_TIMER` (only accepted from Audora's own package), each sentence's book/page/char range in its metadata extras, book/sleep timer/error in the session extras. The reader's `NoPlaybackPort` stand-in is replaced by `MediaControllerPlaybackPort`.
- **P1-07 hooks:** `onPlaybackActive(book, page)` while playing or buffering to play (and on every page change), `onPlaybackInactive()` on pause, stop, error, end and sleep timer; on a background thread.
- **Starts as soon as a sentence is rendered** (`QueueConfig.playbackStartBufferMs = 0`): with Media3's default start buffer (1 s, 2 s after a rebuffer) a run of short sentences never fills it, because the next sentence only renders once the playhead moves, so the player waited in BUFFERING forever. The emulator test found this.
- Wake lock: Media3's `WAKE_MODE_LOCAL` (S3 finding 4), no wake lock of our own. No boot start and no playback resumption from the system (P1-06). `<queries>` for the TTS service intent declared (Build Spec §6; P1-03 needs it to list system voices on Android 11+).
- Tests: JVM tests for the render queue (order, window, cancel, failure, recent cache, close), playlist feeding (placeholder swap, insert before placeholder, finish, trim behind), recovery rules, sleep timer, media ids, page cursor and WAV encoding. Device test (emulator job): the session on a real ExoPlayer with a fake book and voice reads every sentence in order and ends only after the last, starts at a char offset, rebuilds on a voice change, and skips a sentence that fails to render. No new dependencies.

**Known limits:** a 15- or 60-minute screen-off run can only be checked on a phone (below). Logs carry book ids, pages and sentence keys (`p12u3`), never the text.

**Real-device checks (Moto Edge 50 Fusion, dev build; batch with the S1/S3 phone run):** (1) Open an English PDF, press Play: reading starts within a few seconds and the notification shows the title and "Page N of M". (2) Screen off, phone on the table, 15 min (P1-04 done-when), then 60 min (Phase 1 done-when): reading never stops; Share logs afterwards. (3) Lock screen and notification play/pause/next/previous; wired or Bluetooth headset button pauses and resumes; unplugging the headset pauses. ## P1-03 — Speech engines (2026-10-15)
- **`SpeechEngine` contract** (`:speech`): `render(text, voice)` returns 16-bit mono PCM or a typed `SpeechError` (`VoiceUnavailable` with reason UNKNOWN_VOICE / NOT_INSTALLED / NETWORK_ONLY / MODEL_FILES_MISSING, `EngineUnavailable`, `SynthesisFailed`), each with a readable message. Renders are main-safe, serialized per engine and cancellable (the caller gets `CancellationException`, the engine stays usable). `VoiceId`/`EngineId` and `SpeechConfig` live in `:core` so resume anchors can store a voice.
- **Bundled neural voice** (`SherpaSpeechEngine`): sherpa-onnx 1.13.8, 2 threads, one model loaded at a time, loaded on first render. Cancellation stops between sherpa-onnx's internal sentences. Release never frees a model under a running render. The voice is chosen in `speech/bundled-voices.json` (provisional: Piper ljspeech; swap to cori or Kitten by changing one line after the phone rating) and downloaded at build time from sherpa-onnx's release with a SHA-256 check; espeak-ng-data trimmed to en/hi/bn (ADR-014).
- **Phone's own TTS** (`SystemSpeechEngine`): `synthesizeToFile` into the cache, chunked by `getMaxSpeechInputLength()` at sentence/clause/space boundaries; only offline, installed voices; `bestVoice(lang)` says NOT_INSTALLED or NETWORK_ONLY per language when the voice pack is missing. Entry points: `SpeechEngines.bundled(context)` and `SpeechEngines.system(context)`.
- Tests: 44 JVM tests (chunker table, WAV reader, PCM conversion, manifest parsing, asset install incl. a killed copy, both engines over fakes: voice choice, missing packs, chunk join, sample-rate mismatch, errors, cancellation, release during a render). Device tests (emulator job, `:speech:connectedDebugAndroidTest`): the bundled voice renders real speech from the APK, a cancelled render leaves it usable, the system engine renders or reports a typed reason. Build task checked locally: Piper and Kitten unpack and speak in desktop sherpa-onnx; a wrong pin fails the build.
- APK grows by ~92 MB (model, espeak data, sherpa-onnx native libraries). No new libraries.

**Real-device checks (batched into the overnight run, Moto Edge 50 Fusion):** covered by P1-04's playback run: the first sentence starts within ~2 s of Play on a fresh install (model load + espeak copy), and a Hindi book with no Hindi voice pack installed shows the "download it in Text-to-speech output" message.

## P1-01 — Library (2026-10-15)
- **Home is now the library.** "Add PDF" opens the system file picker; Audora keeps a lasting read grant for the file (it is not copied) and lists the book with its title (from the file name), page count once opened, and when it was last opened or added. Most recently used first. Each row's menu has "Remove from library" (asks first; the PDF stays on the phone, the grant is given back).
- **Books are recognized by content, not by location:** fingerprint = SHA-256 of the file size plus its first 1 MiB (`LibraryConfig`), so adding a 1,300-page book costs the same as a 10-page one. Tap to find it."; a lost grant says "Audora can no longer open this file. Tap to pick it again." Tapping opens the picker; the same PDF relinks the book, a different PDF is refused with a clear message. Apps that only lend a file for a moment (no lasting grant) get "Save the PDF to your phone first".

- **Navigation:** Library, Book and Settings are a saveable route string (`AppDestination`), no navigation library; if P1-05 makes the graph deeper, that is when to ask for Navigation Compose. Settings now also shows the app version.
- **Database:** `audora.db` version 1, table `books` with unique URI and unique fingerprint. ## P1-07 — Diagnostics (2026-10-15)
- **Settings → Share logs:** a gear on the home screen opens Settings; "Share logs" zips a `summary.txt` (app version, phone, Android version, unfinished read, last 10 process exits) and the rolling log files into `cacheDir/shared-logs/` and opens the share sheet through a FileProvider limited to that folder. One zip, because every chat and mail app takes a single attachment and text logs compress about tenfold. The log queue is flushed first so the newest lines are included. No PDF text or book titles go into the log or the summary.
- **Process deaths are logged:** at every process start (background thread) Audora reads Android's `ApplicationExitInfo` history and writes each exit not yet logged: kind (SIGNALED, LOW_MEMORY, CRASH, CRASH_NATIVE, ANR, USER_REQUESTED, FREEZER…), signal/exit status, importance (e.g. `FOREGROUND_SERVICE(125)`, the OEM-kill signature), memory and Android's description. Abnormal kinds are warnings. A cursor in `filesDir/diagnostics/state.properties` makes each exit appear once.
- **Unfinished-session detection:** `ReadingSessionTracker` (`:core`) keeps an "audio is playing" marker on disk (atomic write + fsync). A marker found by a new process means the old one died mid-read: the log gets "Reading stopped unexpectedly: book=… page=…; cause: <exit>" and the app shows "Reading stopped unexpectedly — resume?". Paused sessions are not markers, so Android reclaiming a paused app never triggers the prompt.
- **Hooks for later tickets:** P1-04's playback service must call `onPlaybackActive(bookId, page)` on play and page change and `onPlaybackInactive()` on pause, stop, end of book and sleep timer. P1-06 makes "Resume" start playback at the `ResumeAnchor` (`TODO(P1-06)` in `DiagnosticsController`); until then Resume only closes the prompt.
- **Debug builds only:** Settings has "Simulate a reading session", which sets the marker without audio so the prompt and the exit logging can be checked on a phone before P1-04.
- Settings is a second screen switched by state and closed with back; P1-01 replaces it with real navigation. No new dependencies.
- Tests: JVM tests for the state file (round trip, corrupt file, stale temp file, unwritable folder), the tracker (dies while playing, paused, page change, repeated deaths, resolve), startup exit logging (once per exit, levels, cause selection), the zip writer and the summary; an app unit test for the Android reason/importance mapping.

**Real-device checks (Moto Edge 50 Fusion, dev build):** (1) Home → gear → Share logs → pick an app: the zip arrives and holds `summary.txt` plus `audora.log`. (2) Settings → "Simulate a reading session", then App info → Force stop, reopen Audora: the "Reading stopped unexpectedly" prompt names page 14; Share logs again: the log has "Previous process ended: USER_REQUESTED…" and "Reading stopped unexpectedly…".
