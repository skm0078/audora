# ADR 2026-10-29-page-curl: pages curl like paper when they turn

**Status:** Accepted (2026-10-29). Group A of the Moon+ Reader gap plan, approved by the maintainer with three
choices: Curl first and the default, "Turn pages" on by default for text books, and turns made by reading aloud
animate too (quicker). Follows ADR 2026-10-28-page-flip (which listed the curl as a follow-up) and ADR
2026-10-28-text-pages (which had only a slide).

## Context

On the test phone the reader's page turn felt missing: a PDF page slid flat, a text book did not turn at all unless
"Turn pages" was switched on (off by default), and a page turned by reading aloud jumped with no animation. Moon+
Reader, the paid e-reader listeners compare us with, curls the page like paper and lets the reader choose the effect,
its speed and its shading.

## Decision

1. **A page turn is part of the reading look** (`PageTurn` in `ReadingLook.turn`): Curl (default), Slide or None;
   speed Quick, Normal or Slow (0.6x, 1x, 1.6x of 400 ms for a curl, 200 ms for a slide); curl shadow Light, Normal or
   Deep. It is under Page and text, in both views; speed shows unless None, shadow only for Curl.
2. **The curl is 2D geometry on a Canvas** (`core/flip/PageCurl`, drawn by `ui/reader/flip/CurlFrame`): the lifted
   corner is carried to the finger; the page folds along the line halfway between them; the part past the fold is
   cut away (the page beneath shows there with a shadow beside the fold), and mirrored across it as the back of the
   paper, with 12 % of the print showing through and a shade towards the fold. The page is held at its spine: the
   finger is kept within the page's reach of both spine corners, so the paper never tears away. No shader and no 3D,
   so it runs on every phone the app supports (API 31 and up; AGSL would need 33).
3. **PDF pages, by finger** (`FlipState`): a drag from the top or bottom third lifts that corner and carries it up or
   down with the finger; from the middle third the page folds upright, as a whole edge. A turn back curls the page
   before in over the shown one. Let go, and it turns or settles back in the chosen speed, as before (same turn and
   settle rules, ADR 2026-10-28-page-flip). Zoomed in, a drag still pans and never turns. Once a sideways drag has
   begun a turn, the page takes the finger's moves before its vertical scroll sees them, so lifting the corner never
   hands the drag to the scroll part way.
4. **PDF pages, by themselves:** a page change of one that no finger made (reading on, an arrow, an edge tap) plays
   in the same style: the old page curls away over the new one, or the new one curls in over the old. While reading
   aloud it takes 250 ms whatever the speed, so the eye sees the turn while the voice carries on. Without the old
   page's picture in memory (zoomed in), it shows at once.
5. **Text view:** the list is recorded as it draws (a display list, no pixels). A turn first makes that screen a
   picture, moves the list, then curls the picture away over the new screen (on) or curls the new screen in over it
   (back). The list itself never moves, so TalkBack's focus and the sentence highlight stay where they are. A turn
   onto the next page of the book keeps the picture until that page shows. Reading on keeps the last screen of a page
   as a picture while its last sentence is read, so reading's turn onto the next page curls too.
6. **"Turn pages" on by default.** Its saved value moved to a new key: `save()` writes every field, so the old key
   held "off" for anyone who had changed any look setting and the new default would never have reached them.

## Cost (worked out from the code; to be measured on the test phone before release)

- **Memory, PDF:** none more. The pages either side are already drawn ahead (ADR 2026-10-28-page-flip); the curl
  draws those same pictures.
- **Memory, text view:** one picture of the text area during a turn: about 8 MB from the GPU (1080 x ~1900 x 4 bytes),
  or about 4 MB when drawn on the CPU (two bytes a pixel), freed when the turn ends. While reading aloud, held from a
  page's last sentence until the next page shows (seconds), and let go if reading stops.
- **Per frame while turning:** three page-sized fills and two gradient fills on the GPU, plus small polygon maths; the
  same order as the slide. Nothing runs when no page is turning.
- **CPU per turn (text view):** one GPU readback of the screen; on a phone that cannot, one CPU draw of the text
  (a few milliseconds).
- **APK:** about 20 KB of code, no library, no assets. **Cold start:** none.
- **Lighter options, one setting away:** Slide (no picture, no fold) or None (no animation at all).

## Not in this change (follow-ups, not dropped)

- A finger-following curl in the text view: a swipe there plays the whole curl, as the turn needs the next screen
  laid out first. The PDF view follows the finger.
- A realistic paper bend (a cylinder mesh with lighting): heavier per frame; the flat fold matches Moon+'s default.
- Dual pages in landscape (group E of the gap plan).

## Alternatives considered

- **AGSL page-curl shader:** smoother bend, but API 33+ only, and the app supports API 31.
- **A 3D mesh (OpenGL):** the heaviest option, a second rendering path to maintain, and no gain a reader would see at a
  phone's turn speed.
- **Rendering the next text screen offscreen instead of keeping the old one:** needs a second layout of the list per
  turn; keeping the old screen as a picture costs one readback and no layout.

## Consequences

- `ReadingLook` gains `turn`; `turnPages` defaults to on. `PageView` and `Sentences` take the turn and the flip
  config, both defaulted, so other callers are untouched.
- `TextScreens.keepInView` now asks `turnFor` first; `TurnSlide.play` takes its time.
- The release journey gains step 50; step 28 picks Slide explicitly.
